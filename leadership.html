Philosophy

How I lead.

When I joined CodeStax.Ai, I was the only designer. Today there are three designers on my team — all hired, trained, and promoted from intern level.

01 — Hiring

I hire for curiosity, not credentials.

My strongest designers didn't come from premium colleges or fancy bootcamps. They came in hungry, willing to break things, and willing to learn out loud. That matters more than a polished portfolio from someone who stops growing the day they join.

I interview for thinking, not pixels. I'd rather see a rough sketch with sharp reasoning than a beautiful mockup with no "why." Anyone can learn Figma in a month. You can't teach instinct.

02 — Mentoring

1:1s, not performance reviews.

Every designer on my team gets a monthly 1:1 focused on where they want to go, not what I need from them. I review their work, but the conversation is about their growth path, their weak spots, and what unlocks the next level.

One of my designers was taking too long on every task — not because he was slow, but because he didn't know when to stop iterating. I introduced time-boxing as a technique. Productivity went up, anxiety went down.

Feedback that sticks. I don't just point at problems. I sit with the designer and solve it together, so the lesson is absorbed, not memorized. My juniors now do the same for their peers.

03 — Cross-team

I translate between design and engineering.

Being an engineer by degree helps — I can read a dev's frustration and understand what's actually blocking them. Most design-dev conflicts aren't about taste, they're about unclear trade-offs. I surface those trade-offs and align everyone on the call.

I defend the work, not my ego. If a designer on my team has a better solution than mine, theirs ships. If a stakeholder has a valid concern, we incorporate it. If someone's pushing a change that'll hurt users, I push back hard — with data, not opinions.

I take blame, share credit. When things go wrong, it's on me. When things go right, it's the team. This isn't a philosophy — it's how you build people who'll run through walls for the work.

Systems I built

Not just rituals —
actual infrastructure.

01
UI/UX Skill Matrix
A framework mapping every skill a designer needs — from research to systems thinking to visual craft — across four growth levels. Every designer knows exactly where they are and what's next. Growth becomes visible, not vibes-based.
02
Weekly Insight Sessions
Every week, one team member presents a research article, product teardown, or design principle they've been thinking about. Rotates across the team. Side effect: we read more now. The sessions have surfaced ideas that directly influenced live product decisions.
03
Monthly Creative Jam — No-AI Zone
Once a month we lock the AI tools away. Pure creativity — drawing, sketching, hand prototyping, mind maps. In an era where AI can generate anything in 10 seconds, I wanted to make sure my team still knew how to think with a pencil.
04
Weekly Design Review Rituals
Critique culture is non-negotiable. We review each other's work weekly, structured around the problem — not the pixels. Every designer is expected to present their thinking, not just their output. Juniors learn to give feedback too, not just receive it.

AI
AI & Automation

I was doing this
before it was
trendy.

Most designers discovered AI around ChatGPT's release. I was already there — running ComfyUI locally, building custom model pipelines, generating consistent AI influencer characters and video content for marketing — before NanoBanana or similar tools existed. Here's what I've actually done.
Workflow Automation
n8n — Internal automations that actually saved time
Built multiple n8n workflows for internal team productivity: design review → ticket creation (Figma comments → Jira tasks automatically), content repurposing pipelines (long-form → social-ready carousels), asset generation triggers (brief submitted → AI-generated moodboards delivered to team), and social media scheduling integrations for the LinkedIn growth project. These weren't experiments — they ran in production.
Generative AI
ComfyUI — Consistent AI creative assets
Generated consistent AI influencer characters for brand marketing using ComfyUI with custom node workflows. Consistent character across different poses, outfits, and scenes — before dedicated consistency tools existed. Also built image→video workflows for marketing content without After Effects.
Design-to-Code
Vibe coding — From design intent to working frontend
Built functional frontend prototypes using AI-assisted coding (Cursor, v0, Claude). Experimenting with Figma → React pipelines to reduce dev handoff friction. Prototyped internal tools end-to-end — design, code, deploy — to validate ideas before committing full dev time.
AI Fashion
Fashion & product generation
Experimented with generating consistent apparel on consistent models for e-commerce and marketing use cases. Built pipelines for product shot generation and editorial fashion imagery — significantly faster than traditional photography for concept validation.
AI Video
Marketing video without video production
Built workflows: reference image → multiple generated poses → stitched video sequences. Produced fashion shoots, branded content, and product demos without a camera or After Effects. Used for CodeStax marketing content in production.
Current Obsession
Design systems as prompt libraries
If a design system defines components, tokens, and patterns — a well-structured one should let AI generate new screens that are automatically on-brand. Experimenting with this at CodeStax. Early results: promising. The goal isn't to replace designers — it's to make the repetitive parts near-instant so designers can focus on strategy and research.
