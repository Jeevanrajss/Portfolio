Context

Myanmar's largest bank. From scratch.

KBZ Bank is one of the largest banks in Myanmar, with over 10 million active users on their digital platform. CodeStax partnered with a core banking technology company to rebuild their entire system — web app, responsive web, and native mobile — from the ground up.

I led the design. Alone, initially — before we built the team around it.

The Problem

Functional but broken at scale.

The existing KBZ experience was functional but heavy. Users were buried under steps, menus, and legacy patterns from a decade of incremental additions. A simple money transfer took 6 screens. Key actions were hidden behind nested menus. The system worked — but it wasn't fast, and it wasn't scaling.

"The business brief was clear: modernize the entire platform, reduce friction, and build a system that can grow."
The Constraints

This is the wild part.

Myanmar went through a major political crisis during the project. Internet connectivity was unstable, parts of the country were under unrest, and direct access to users was impossible. No interviews. No usability tests. No field research. For a project of this scale, that's a nightmare.

  • Dual-language system — English + Burmese, with very different reading patterns and UI conventions
  • Cultural UX expectations — closer to Chinese/Singaporean banking UX — users wanted everything visible, not hidden behind progressive disclosure
  • Architectural constraints — designing within a partner's core banking architecture with non-negotiable data structures
  • Zero user access — no direct research possible due to political instability and connectivity issues
My Approach

When you can't talk to users, you get creative.

Approach 01
Competitor benchmarking as research substitute
I studied 20+ banking apps across Asia — ICICI, HDFC, AYA Bank, UOB, DBS, and KBZ's existing Pay app. Mapped common patterns, flagged cultural UX differences, and built a reference library of what "good" looks like in this region.
Approach 02
Data from secondary sources
Play Store reviews, forum complaints, product feedback logs from KBZ's existing app — I mined everything available to rebuild the picture of who the user is and what's breaking. Treated negative reviews as unprompted usability sessions.
Approach 03
Stakeholders as proxy research
PMs, partner architects, and KBZ liaisons became my proxy research channel. I ran structured alignment sessions to extract real usage patterns, edge cases, and regulatory constraints. Every decision needed to be justified with something beyond my own assumption.
The Process

Architecture before aesthetics.

Information architecture first. Before touching visuals, I restructured the entire app hierarchy. Flattened nested menus. Surfaced high-frequency actions. Built a navigation model that matched regional mental models — visible primary actions, bottom bar always accessible.

The payment flow rewrite — the most critical change. 6 steps became 3:
  • Select beneficiary — with smart recency + favorites surfaced first
  • Enter amount + note
  • Confirm & pay

Scheduling, recurring transfers, and bill pay were folded into the same flow instead of being separate journeys.

System-level thinking. I designed a shared component library that could scale across 3000+ screens without breaking. Master components, overrides, and styles were structured for reuse — the foundation of what would later become a proper design system. Both English and Burmese versions shared the same components with language-specific spacing rules.

Cultural calibration. Reduced hover states (Asian mobile-first users don't hover). Moved primary actions to bottom sheets. Increased tap targets significantly. Kept visual density higher than Western standards because users expected it — empty space reads as "missing content."

Learnings

What this project changed in me.

Learning 01
"Research isn't a checklist — it's a mindset."
When you can't run interviews, you still need to understand the user. You just have to be more creative about where you find the signal. Secondary sources, competitor teardowns, proxy stakeholders — all valid. Constraint produces creativity.
Learning 02
"Cultural UX is real — and it matters more than you think."
What feels "clean" in San Francisco feels "empty" in Yangon. Defaulting to Western design norms on an Asian product is how you ship something nobody wants to use. Every design decision has cultural assumptions baked in — the job is to find them before they become problems.
Learning 03
"You can't design 3000 screens one at a time."
You design the system, and the screens design themselves. This project made me a systems thinker. The shift from "what does this screen look like" to "what system produces these screens consistently" is the most important transition a designer can make.
