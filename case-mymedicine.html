Context

Born in the middle of a crisis.

MyMedicine was born during Myanmar's COVID lockdowns and political unrest simultaneously. Hospitals were overwhelmed. Clinics were hard to reach. People couldn't physically get to a doctor — and digital healthcare wasn't really a thing yet for most of the country.

The opportunity was clear: bring healthcare to people when they couldn't go to healthcare.

It started as a simple doctor appointment booking app. It became a full healthcare ecosystem.

The Problem

Access was the core issue.

Lockdowns kept people home. Political instability made travel risky. Hospitals were under pressure. Phone-based appointments didn't scale. No unified digital health service existed — doctor booking, ordering medicine, scheduling a lab test were all separate manual processes.

"The business brief started narrow — build a doctor booking app. The moment it hit the market, users wanted more: Pharmacy. Labs. Nurses. Hospitals. Corporate wellness."
The Constraints

Small team. Growing scope. Crisis market.

  • Designing for a market in crisis — intermittent internet, political chaos, low digital literacy for healthcare apps
  • No direct user research access — same Myanmar constraint as KBZ, couldn't run live sessions
  • Scope that kept growing — each new service had different logic, different partners, different flows
  • Small team, massive surface — me + 1 designer covering mobile app, responsive web, AND admin portals for every service
  • One design system across radically different verticals — booking a doctor ≠ ordering medicine ≠ scheduling a nurse, but all had to feel like one product
My Approach

Design the ecosystem, not the next feature.

Approach 01
Research even without direct access
Studied Halodoc, Practo, 1mg, Apollo 24/7. Mapped what worked in similar emerging markets. Mined Myanmar health forums and Play Store reviews as proxy research.
Approach 02
Design the ecosystem from day one
When the business said "add e-pharmacy", I didn't design an e-pharmacy screen. I redesigned the home experience to accommodate 6+ services — so every new vertical slotted in without rebuilding the app.
Approach 03
Reusable components from day one
With 2 designers covering mobile, web, and admin panels, we had no choice but to build a component system early. Service cards, booking flows, payment screens, confirmation states — all shared.
The Process

One grammar. Six services. Three surfaces.

Home as a service hub. Redesigned the home screen around service discovery. Each vertical got its own entry point but shared consistent card patterns, booking flows, and checkout experiences.

Booking flows: one grammar, many contexts. Doctor, lab, nurse, hospital bookings — all follow the same 4-step logic: Browse → Select → Schedule → Confirm. Users learn it once, apply it everywhere.

E-pharmacy. Full pharmacy flow — search, prescription upload, cart, delivery. Healthcare-specific trust signals (prescription verification, genuine medicine badges) because medicine is not a shoe.

Admin portals for every service. Each vertical has its own operational backend. I designed all of them to share the same admin design language so ops teams could switch contexts without retraining.

AI chatbot integration. Designed a conversational layer where users can ask questions, get recommendations, or trigger bookings through chat without leaving their current flow.

Learnings

What crisis design taught me.

Learning 01
"Design for the ecosystem, not the feature."
Designing for today's brief means rebuilding in 6 months. Design for where the product is going. The architecture decisions I made on day 1 — home as a service hub, shared booking grammar — meant every new service slotted in smoothly.
Learning 02
"Small teams + big scope = systems thinking is not optional."
Two designers can't ship a mobile app, web app, and admin portals for 6 services without a shared component library. The system carries the team.
Learning 03
"Healthcare is trust-first."
Our users weren't looking for polish — they needed something that worked under bad internet, political instability, and health anxiety. Reliability beat beauty every time. Every design decision got filtered through "does this make the user trust us more, or less?"
