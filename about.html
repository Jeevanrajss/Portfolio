2016 — The beginning

A photo edit. A chess academy. A career.

It started because my brother needed better posters for his chess academy. I had free time in college, a pirated copy of Photoshop, and a YouTube algorithm that would teach me anything for free. I started making banners, brochures, pamphlets, logos for local clients in Dindigul.

No formal design education. No bootcamp. Just me and the internet. Self-taught is a nice way of saying I made every mistake before anyone could stop me — and learned faster because of it.

"I didn't choose design. Design was just the thing that made me feel like I was solving something real."
2018 — First full-time job

Hitasoft. White-label products. Adobe XD. A team of 4.

Joined Hitasoft Technology Solutions in Madurai as a UI Designer. White-label company — Amazon-like marketplaces, Airbnb-like rentals, Uber-like rides, OLX clones, live chat. All customized for clients across industries.

The whole team was designing in Photoshop. Adobe XD had just launched. I was the fresher who convinced my manager to switch. Made everyone learn it by showing what prototyping could do — clients clicking through flows instead of staring at static screens. That was my first lesson in design advocacy.

Six months in, I was leading a team of 4 — two UI developers included. Learned After Effects, motion micro-interactions, rebranded the company using 3D objects and motion graphics. Left April 2019 after 10 months, having learned more than I expected.

2019 — Going solo at a startup

Parampriti. Solo designer. 15+ clients. One Lenco watch.

Joined Parampriti Web as the only designer at a startup studio in Chennai. Shipped websites and web apps for a wide range of clients — PMIST (Periyar Maniammai Institute), Periyar Thidal, construction brands (Polux, Krishna, Landmark, Urbando), and Lenco Watches, a sub-brand of Ajanta Clocks.

The Lenco e-commerce project got huge appreciation from both client and users. The parallax design I built for it was one of the most appreciated things I'd done at that point. First time I felt like design had a real multiplier effect on business.

The PMIST university website was another landmark — hundreds of pages, completely revamped with modern, minimalistic design within their brand guidelines. Took about a month. Being the only designer at a startup teaches you speed, ownership, and tolerance for ambiguity.

2020 — doodleblue

Pandemic start. Rescue projects. A team of juniors.

Joined doodleblue in December 2020 — right in the middle of COVID. Worked remotely on multiple projects from start to end. Two of the most memorable ones came to me after previous designers had been rejected.

Mr.Med — an e-pharmacy web app. Two designers' rounds had been rejected by the client. I reviewed the rejections, understood the pattern, redesigned around the user's emotional state (anxious, in a hurry, needing trust). Third round approved. Shipped live.

BeyondSkool — a special education platform for kids with learning needs. Again, previous rounds rejected. I designed three separate experiences within one product — for students, parents, and teachers — without conflating them. Approved and shipped.

"Rescue projects taught me more than clean starts. Two rounds of rejection is two rounds of free insight. Read the failures first."

Also mentored 3 junior designers here, introduced structured handoff and review processes. Left September 2021.

2021 — CodeStax.Ai

3000 screens. A SaaS from zero. A team I built.

Joined CodeStax.Ai (then Trinom Digital) in October 2021 as the only designer. First major project: KBZ Bank core banking redesign — one of Myanmar's largest banks, 10M+ active users, working with a core banking partner to rebuild the entire platform from scratch.

Myanmar's political crisis made direct user research impossible. Internet was unstable, travel was out of the question. I rebuilt my research approach from secondary sources — Play Store reviews, competitor benchmarking across 20+ Asian banking apps, stakeholder workshops to extract ground truth. I designed 3000+ screens across web, mobile, and responsive web, in both English and Burmese.

Then FlowStax — CodeStax's first in-house product. A no-code workflow builder that digitized decades of manual paper-based bank operations. I was the sole designer, 0 to 1, from concept to design system. The company's first component library. Now processing 2M+ tokens.

Then MyMedicine — a healthcare app born during Myanmar's COVID+political crisis. Started as doctor booking, scaled to 6+ services (pharmacy, lab, nurse, hospital, corporate, AI chatbot) with a 2-person design team. Mobile + web + admin portals for every service.

Alongside all of this: hired, trained, and promoted 3 designers from intern → UX Designer II. Built the company's first design system, first skill matrix, weekly design rituals, monthly no-AI creative jams. Co-led LinkedIn from 0 to 1000+ followers in 4 months.

Now — What I'm focused on

AI-assisted design. Systems as prompt libraries. What comes next.

I'm currently focused on how AI changes the designer's job — specifically, how design systems can become prompt libraries that let AI generate on-brand screens automatically. Experimenting with this at CodeStax. Early results are promising.

I've been running n8n automations to free up my team's time, working on vibe-coded prototypes that go directly from design intent to working frontend, and generating AI creative assets (images, video, influencer consistency) using ComfyUI pipelines I built before most tools existed.

"The designer's job isn't going away. It's shifting. Strategy, research, systems thinking, and taste get more valuable. Pushing pixels gets less."

I'm looking for my next mission — a role where design has real weight in product decisions, a team I can grow with and lead, a product where complexity is the feature, not the bug. Fintech, SaaS, healthcare, or anywhere that needs systems thinking at scale.

What drives me

How I actually think.

⬡
Systems before screens
You can't design 3000 screens one at a time. You design the system, and the screens design themselves. This is the only thing that scales.
◎
Research is a mindset, not a checklist
I designed for 10M users in Myanmar without ever meeting one. Research isn't just interviews — it's finding signal anywhere it exists. Secondary sources, competitor teardowns, proxy stakeholders.
↑
Mentor by showing, not telling
I sit with designers and solve together. The lesson is absorbed, not memorized. Feedback without collaboration is just criticism.
✦
Cultural UX is real
What feels "clean" in San Francisco feels "empty" in Yangon. Design norms are cultural. I've learned this the hard way and it's changed how I approach every product.
