How I hire

I hire for curiosity, not credentials. My strongest designers didn't come from premium colleges. They came in hungry, willing to break things, and willing to learn out loud. Anyone can learn Figma in a month. You can't teach instinct.

How I mentor

Monthly 1:1s focused on where they want to go, not what I need from them. I sit with designers and solve problems together — lessons absorbed, not memorized. Introduced time-boxing for a designer who was over-iterating. Productivity up, anxiety down.

Systems I built

UI/UX Skill Matrix — maps every skill across four levels so growth is visible, not vibes-based. Weekly Insight Sessions — one team member presents a research article each week. Monthly Creative Jam (No-AI Zone) — lock the tools away and think with a pencil.

How I lead across teams

Being an engineer by degree helps — I can read a dev's frustration and understand what's actually blocking them. I defend the work, not my ego. If a designer on my team has a better solution, theirs ships. Take blame, share credit.

FAQ

What is your expertise?
+
Turning messy business logic into products that actually ship. Designing systems that scale across thousands of screens. Leading small teams without losing the craft. Redesigning products that other designers gave up on. And building design into a function, not just a service.
How do you think about product strategy?
+
Start with the business goal. Reverse-engineer the user behavior needed to hit it. Design the shortest path between the two. Most "design problems" are actually unclear goals in a trenchcoat.
How do you measure design success?
+
Three layers, in order: did the user get what they came for, did the product grow, did the business win. If only one of three is happening, something's broken upstream.
What's your take on AI in design?
+
AI is really good at the boring parts. Great designers lean in. I've been automating creative workflows with n8n, ComfyUI, and custom pipelines since before it was a trend. The job isn't going away — it's shifting. Strategy, research, taste, and systems thinking get more valuable. Pushing pixels gets less.
How do you handle design-dev disagreements?
+
I look for the problem behind the opinion. Most disagreements aren't about taste — they're about unstated goals. Surface those, and the "right" answer usually becomes obvious. Being an engineer by degree helps — I can read a dev's frustration and understand what's actually blocking them.
Are you open to remote or relocation?
+
Full-time is my primary focus. Open to remote, Bengaluru, or global relocation. I'll consider contract for the right fintech/healthcare/SaaS problem. Not currently looking for part-time or agency work.
